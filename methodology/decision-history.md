## DEC-001 — Refocus the assessment on selected European critical-infrastructure operations

**Decision date:** 13 October 2026  
**Decision status:** Approved in principle; implementation pending  
**Related project steps:** 270–271  
**Decision type:** Research scope and publication strategy  
**Repository baseline:** `f76575f8c19dc17123f5bd708cc238c2359d3420`

### Previous objective

The project originally sought to assess Russian state-linked cyber activity affecting European organisations across countries, sectors and threat actors between 1 January 2025 and 30 September 2026.

The original scope required extensive collection to support broader judgements about targeting patterns, technical tradecraft, operational evolution and implications for European security.

### Decision

Refocus the final publication on a comparative assessment of three selected cases involving European critical infrastructure and supporting industrial suppliers.

**Proposed working title:**

*Russian State-Linked Cyber Operations Against European Critical Infrastructure: A Comparative Assessment of Selected Cases, 2025–2026*

**Revised primary intelligence question:**

What do three selected, publicly documented cyber operations reveal about Russian state-linked targeting of European critical infrastructure, the methods employed, observed operational consequences, attribution uncertainty and defensive implications during 2025–2026?

### Selected case studies

1. **Polish energy-sector attacks, December 2025:** INC-002, INC-003 and INC-005, examined as components of a coordinated operation rather than independent campaigns.
2. **Ukrainian industrial supplier campaign, January–February 2025:** INC-008, including reported targeting, technical activity and suspected critical-infrastructure supply-chain objectives.
3. **Swedish district-heating incident, spring 2025:** INC-006, subject to explicit limitations concerning incident dating, technical evidence and attribution.

Other previously collected incidents remain in the repository as supporting or out-of-scope research. Their inclusion in the research dataset does not imply inclusion in the final publication.

### Rationale

The original Europe-wide scope was disproportionately broad for a single-researcher portfolio assessment and risked creating an open-ended collection exercise without a defined analytical endpoint.

A comparative case-study approach allows more thorough examination of primary evidence, competing attribution assessments, technical findings and operational consequences.

The revision is intended to improve analytical depth and publication feasibility without lowering evidentiary standards.

### Geographic and temporal boundaries

The assessment period remains **1 January 2025 to 30 September 2026**.

The proposed geographic scope explicitly includes Ukraine, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Norway and relevant European partner countries. The final geographic wording remains subject to approval during implementation.

Incident occurrence dates must remain distinguishable from publication and disclosure dates.

### Methodological continuity

The project retains:

- Five Priority Intelligence Requirements, revised to address the selected cases.
- Admiralty source reliability and information credibility evaluations.
- Explicit High, Moderate and Low analytical confidence assessments.
- Separation of observed activity, reported claims, official attribution and analytical inference.
- Independent corroboration and consideration of competing explanations.
- Evidence-supported MITRE ATT&CK mappings.
- Source-to-claim-to-incident traceability.
- Preservation of unresolved questions and evidence limitations.

Russian state direction must not be inferred solely from language, political alignment or unverified claims of responsibility.

### Publication boundaries

The assessment will not claim that three selected cases represent the frequency, geographic distribution or overall prevalence of Russian cyber operations across Europe.

The report must distinguish attempted compromise, confirmed compromise, destructive activity and verified operational disruption.

The Swedish case must be presented in proportion to the limited publicly available technical evidence. Material deficiencies may require reconsideration of its role without automatically adding a replacement case.

Additional research should be targeted towards evidence that could materially change attribution, operational-impact findings or central analytical judgements.

### Repository and publication strategy

The existing repository and Git commit history will be preserved.

Existing source, claim, incident, TTP and collection-lead records will not be deleted merely because they fall outside the revised publication scope.

The intended outputs are:

1. A professionally presented GitHub research repository.
2. A full evidence-led CTI assessment in PDF format.
3. A concise executive intelligence brief.
4. A LinkedIn publication introducing the findings and linking to the supporting research.

### Implementation and change control

This decision records the approved strategic direction. It does not, by itself, amend existing intelligence requirements, collection criteria or repository presentation.

Those changes require separate review and explicit authorisation.

The revised scope will be considered fully implemented only after the relevant methodology and project presentation files have been updated and verified.
