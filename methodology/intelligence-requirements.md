
# Intelligence Requirements

**Project:** Russian State-Linked Cyber Activity in Europe  
**Assessment Period:** 1 January 2025 – 30 September 2026  
**Status:** Approved for collection planning  
**Methodology:** CTI & OSINT Analytical Standard v1.0

## 1. Primary Intelligence Question

What does publicly available evidence indicate about the objectives, targeting patterns, technical tradecraft and evolution of Russian state-linked cyber operations affecting European organisations between January 2025 and September 2026, and what are the implications for European security?

## 2. Assessment Scope

### Geographic Scope

- European Union member states
- United Kingdom
- Norway
- Relevant European partner countries

Activity involving organisations outside this geographic scope may be considered where it directly affects European targets or provides necessary campaign context.

### Threat Actor Scope

The assessment includes:

- Actors linked to Russian state agencies by documented attribution.
- Cyber operations attributed to Russian state-linked threat groups.
- Proxy actors where a specific relationship to Russian state interests or direction is supported by evidence.

Russian-speaking cybercriminals, politically aligned hacktivists and groups merely claiming Russian affiliation are not automatically considered state-linked.

### Assessment Period

1 January 2025 – 30 September 2026.

Earlier activity may be used as historical context but must not be counted as activity occurring within the assessment period.

## 3. Priority Intelligence Requirements

### PIR-01: Threat Actors and Attribution

Which Russian state-linked actors and documented proxies conducted or were credibly reported to have conducted cyber operations affecting European organisations?

**Key Intelligence Questions:**

- Which actors were identified?
- What evidence supports their attribution?
- Which campaigns can be reliably associated with them?
- Where does attribution remain disputed or uncertain?

### PIR-02: Targeting and Objectives

Which countries, sectors and organisations were targeted, and what objectives can be established?

**Key Intelligence Questions:**

- Which geographic areas and sectors appear most frequently in documented reporting?
- What evidence indicates espionage, disruption, prepositioning or other objectives?
- Are apparent targeting patterns affected by reporting bias?

### PIR-03: Technical Tradecraft

Which tactics, techniques and procedures were documented during the assessment period?

**Key Intelligence Questions:**

- Which initial access methods were observed?
- Which tools, malware families and vulnerabilities were documented?
- Which techniques can be mapped to MITRE ATT&CK?
- Are there supported changes in tradecraft over time?
- What detection opportunities arise from the technical evidence?

### PIR-04: Competing Perspectives and Evidence

How do official Russian, European, Ukrainian and independent accounts differ regarding relevant operations?

**Key Intelligence Questions:**

- What claims and counterclaims were publicly issued?
- What independently observable evidence supports or contradicts them?
- Which attribution claims remain unverified?
- Are different reports independently corroborated or derived from the same original source?

### PIR-05: Trends and Defensive Implications

What supported patterns emerge from the evidence, and what implications do they have for European organisations?

**Key Intelligence Questions:**

- Which recurring behaviours are supported by the collected incidents?
- What limitations affect trend interpretation?
- Which security controls, telemetry and detection opportunities are relevant?
- What indicators or developments should be monitored in the following 6–12 months?

## 4. Analytical Boundaries

The assessment will not:

- Assume Russian state involvement solely from language, geography or political alignment.
- Treat claims of responsibility as proof of operational involvement.
- Assume increased activity solely because more incidents were publicly reported.
- Present historical actor TTPs as observed behaviour in a specific incident.
- Treat official attribution or denial as independently proven without examining supporting evidence.
- Present unsupported predictions as established intelligence findings.

## 5. Intended Outputs

1. Full evidence-led CTI assessment.
2. Structured incident and source datasets.
3. Technical TTP analysis with MITRE ATT&CK mappings.
4. Documented analytical judgements with confidence assessments.
5. Concise professional intelligence brief for LinkedIn publication.

## 6. Methodological Reference

This project follows CTI & OSINT Analytical Standard v1.0.

Source reliability and information credibility will be evaluated separately using the Admiralty Code (A–F / 1–6).

Final analytical judgements will use High, Moderate or Low confidence, with supporting rationale and acknowledged limitations.
